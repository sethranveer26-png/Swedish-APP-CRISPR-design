Start here: a two-letter typo in one gene

Familial Alzheimer’s disease can be caused by a single dominant mutation. This project designs, entirely on a computer, a CRISPR guide RNA that targets the Swedish APP mutant allele while differing from the healthy allele at two letters, and proposes delivering the editor to the brain with a lipid nanoparticle instead of viruses.

100% computational · built in Benchling · not tested in the lab or in animals

Builds on György et al. 2018 and Cao et al. 2025. The guide analysis and the delivery proposal are this project’s own work. Scroll to zoom in; drag the image to rotate it.

Why two letters matter

In people with the Swedish mutation (KM670/671NL), one mutant copy (allele) of the APP gene is enough to cause disease. Enzymes cut the APP protein; beta-secretase and then gamma-secretase release amyloid-beta. The mutation sits next to the beta-secretase cut site, so the mutant protein is cut too easily and too much amyloid-beta is made.

Research question: can a computer-designed guide RNA tell the mutant allele from the healthy one by two letters, and could a barrier-crossing nanoparticle carry the editor to the brain?

Strategy, following György et al. 2018: disrupt only the mutant allele rather than correct it, leaving the healthy copy working.

Finding the target in 297,579 letters

The human APP gene record NG_007376 sits on chromosome 21. It was imported into Benchling, and the exon 16 region around the mutation was isolated into two working files: healthy (wild-type) and Swedish.

The healthy protein reads E-V-K-M-D-A-E-F-R at this spot; the Swedish version reads E-V-N-L-D-A-E-F-R.

Two letters, one protein change

Orange: the two mutated letters (positions 48–49 in the working file). Yellow: the TGG landing site (PAM, positions 50–52).

A guide that reads the difference

The guide is a 20-letter address; Cas9 cuts only where it matches and a short PAM (here TGG) sits next to it. The seed region, the letters nearest the PAM, is where a mismatch matters most.

Guide 5′-GGAGATCTCTGAAGTGAATC-3′ + PAM TGG

The TGG PAM is present in both alleles. The discrimination comes from the two seed mismatches, not from a new PAM. Only one usable SpCas9 guide covering the mutation was found in the analysed window.

Simulated cut and frameshift

Cas9 cuts 3 letters before the PAM, between letters 46 and 47. As an illustration of one possible repair outcome, a one-letter insertion shifts the reading frame:

E V N L D A E F
↓ +1 letter ↓
E V I S G C R I P T * stop

A stop signal would prevent that copy from making full-length mutant protein.

Real repair produces a mix of insertions and deletions, and not all of them break the gene. This is a simulation, not a prediction of results.

Delivery: a proposal

No source tests this particle with Cas9 mRNA or with this guide.

Original study: AAV9 viruses. SpCas9 is about 4.1 kb and one AAV holds about 4.7 kb, so editors like this are commonly split or delivered in parts. Proposed: a lipid nanoparticle carrying SpCas9 mRNA plus the guide; mRNA is short-lived, so the editor works for days, not permanently.

About the project

Familial Alzheimer’s disease can run in families because of a single dominant mutation. In the Swedish APP mutation, two letters of the APP gene change, the protein is cut too easily, and too much amyloid-beta is made. A person has one mutant and one healthy copy, so a therapy would need to switch off the mutant copy and spare the healthy one.

What was done

  1. Imported the real human APP gene record into Benchling and built healthy and Swedish versions of the region.
  2. Scanned for CRISPR landing sites and found one usable guide covering the mutation, with both letter differences in its seed region.
  3. Checked it with Benchling and CRISPOR, including the genome-wide off-target list.
  4. Simulated a cut and a one-letter repair that shifts the reading frame.
  5. Specified the RNA cargo and proposed a lipid nanoparticle for delivery.

What is original, and what is not

The approach follows György et al. 2018, which delivered the editor with viruses. The guide analysis and the lipid-nanoparticle proposal, based on a formulation from Cao et al. 2025, are this project’s own work.

What would come next

Test the guide in cells carrying the Swedish mutation, measure how often the healthy allele is also cut, check off-target sites by sequencing, then test a nanoparticle carrying Cas9 mRNA and this guide in a mouse model.

Lipid nanoparticles: the delivery goal

Goal: get Cas9 mRNA and the guide RNA into neurons without viruses, so the editor works for a few days and then disappears. Proposal: no source tests this with Cas9 or this guide

How the particle would work

  1. Cas9 mRNA and the guide RNA are packed inside a lipid shell.
  2. The particle is injected into the blood and carries a brain-targeting surface (a Tat peptide).
  3. It crosses the blood-brain barrier and is taken into a neuron.
  4. The RNA escapes the cell’s internal compartment, and the cell reads the mRNA to make Cas9.
  5. Cas9 and the guide find the Swedish allele and cut it. The mRNA then fades within days.

What the published result shows, and does not show

In mice, with reporter mRNA (not Cas9), the particle gave over 50-fold more brain mRNA translation than an FDA-approved LNP, and about 13% of neurons showed the editing signal after one intravenous dose of Cre mRNA. It reached neurons, astrocytes and microglia without preference.

Limitations

  • Everything is computational. No lab, cell or animal experiments were done.
  • Two seed mismatches reduce, but may not eliminate, cutting of the healthy allele. Only experiments such as sequencing could measure this.
  • The 50-fold and 13% figures come from reporter mRNAs in mice, not Cas9 mRNA plus guide, and not humans.
  • The OS4T particle reached neurons, astrocytes and microglia without preference.
  • Off-target tools cannot prove safety.
  • How many neurons would need editing to affect disease is unknown. The amyloid hypothesis is debated, and the Swedish mutation affects very few families.
  • The Benchling and CRISPOR score labels are still to be confirmed.
  • This is not medical advice.
